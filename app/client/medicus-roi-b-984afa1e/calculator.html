How this calculator works

Three things change when a practice moves to Medicus, each measured at the reference practice: ten minutes of slowdowns and waits avoided in every four-hour clinical session, three minutes saved at each of two logins a clinician makes a day, and half a full-time administrator's week released by automated document handling. The calculator scales each of those to the list size, staffing and working pattern you enter, values the clinical hours at locum and agency replacement rates and admin hours at a loaded wage, and adds the list price of any add-on a native Medicus function retires.

The time is shown as hours, split into clinical and non-clinical, and is not converted into money: how a practice uses it is its own decision, and most of it is usually absorbed into a better working day rather than banked. Direct savings are different: they are the add-on costs that stop once a native Medicus function replaces the add-on, listed for each organisation that pays today. Every time figure is "up to": the reference practice's own experience scaled to your inputs, which is the top of the range to expect. Press any i for the working behind a figure.

Basis and limits
  • Source

    Built from the measured impact of Medicus at a live NHS practice (the reference practice) with 8 FTE GPs, 3 FTE practice nurses and 0.5 FTE of admin time released across 12,310 registered patients. Time saved per login and per session, and the admin time released, are that practice's own figures. A session is a four-hour clinic (a half day of about 24 ten-minute appointments), not a single consultation, and the ten minutes per session is the practice's own conservative estimate of time lost to slow screens and system waits rather than a timed measurement. It is the least evidenced of the three inputs, which is one reason every figure is presented as "up to".

  • Staffing presets

    Reference practice ratios give one GP FTE per 1,539 patients and one practice nurse FTE per 4,103. The national average preset uses NHS England's general practice workforce figures for February 2026 (28,895 FTE fully qualified GPs and 16,728 FTE nurses, a count that includes nurse practitioners and nursing associates) against 63,789,531 registered patients, or about 2,210 patients per GP and 3,810 per nurse. The reference practice is better staffed than average, so its ratios give the higher figure: a single practice starts from the reference ratios, and a network or region starts from the national average because no whole system can be assumed to match one well-staffed practice. Other clinical staff (pharmacists, physiotherapists, paramedics and the other ARRS roles, plus healthcare assistants) are not counted, which makes the clinical time figure conservative, particularly for a PCN. NHS England counts an FTE as 37.5 hours; the model follows the reference practice in counting one as four clinical days.

  • Networks and regions

    For a PCN, federation or ICB the same model is applied to the total registered population and the number of practices entered, and assumes every practice entered moves to Medicus, so count only the practices in scope. The result is a steady state once they are all live, not a phased figure. Clinical staffing is estimated from the total list at the chosen ratio (national average by default), admin time released follows the total list, and add-on costs are estimated for a practice of average list size and multiplied by the number of practices using each tool; Accurx add-ons are priced at the G-Cloud 15 tier for the whole list in scope, as a network or ICB would buy. Practices within a network vary widely in size, systems and funding, so the result is an aggregate, not a sum of individual practice figures.

  • Reading the figures

    The efficiency is shown as hours, split into clinical time (GPs and practice nurses) and non-clinical time (admin and document handling). It is not converted into money, and the model makes no assumption about how a practice uses the time: more appointments, shorter days, less locum cover or simply a calmer working day are all possible, and most of it is usually absorbed into a better working environment rather than banked. Every figure is shown as "up to": the reference practice's own experience scaled to the inputs entered, which is the top of the range to expect rather than a forecast. A practice that already runs lean, or has faster hardware and fewer logins, will see less. Direct savings are different: they are the annual cost of add-ons that native Medicus functions replace, listed separately for each organisation that pays today, and they depend on those contracts actually being ended.

  • Plausibility checks

    Two outside figures sit alongside the reference practice's experience. The BMA's survey of doctors put time lost to inadequate IT at 13.5 million hours a year, the equivalent of almost 8,000 full-time doctors or roughly 5% of medical time; the model's 26 minutes a clinical day is 5.4% of an eight-hour day. And the admin time released, 0.406 FTE per 10,000 patients, is 3.4% of the national general practice admin workforce of 76,845 FTE across 63.8 million patients. Neither proves the figures for any one practice, but both put them in a credible range.

  • Annual hours

    Hours a week are multiplied by 52 working weeks to give the annual figure. That makes no allowance for leave or bank holidays; set 45 weeks under "Weeks a year" to allow for them.

  • Possible overlap

    Login time and session slowdowns are counted separately, as the reference practice counted them. Some of the login saving may already sit inside the slowdown figure, so the two together should be read as an upper estimate.

  • Native functions and add-ons

    The reference practice used Medicus's built-in functions to retire AccuRx, Docman, Docman Connect and ICE, and cut its texting spend by defaulting to email. The slide gave no costs, so each add-on is estimated from a public price list. Patient messaging uses Accurx's two-way patient messaging module on G-Cloud 15 (September 2026), priced by total list size: 38p a patient a year for lists under 15,000, 29p from 15,000, 26p from 90,000 and 23p from 800,000, ex VAT and excluding message fragments. Batch messaging and reminders, questionnaires and video are separate modules on the same list (27p, 27p and 23p at the smallest tier), so a practice that pays for Accurx's Communication bundle (£1.14 a patient) should enter that figure. Online consultation uses Accurx Patient Triage on the same list (38p a patient under 90,000, 29p and 24p above); OneAdvanced lists PATCHS at £0.45 to £0.90 a patient on G-Cloud 14. Booking starts at zero because the add-on varies by practice; Accurx appointment self-book lists 51p a patient. SMS is estimated at £0.51 a patient a year, around 17 message fragments at Accurx's 3p G-Cloud 15 rate; North East and North Cumbria ICB treats practices above £60 per 1,000 patients a month (£0.72 a patient a year) as high spenders. Self check-in and patient calling screens use Engage Health Systems' G-Cloud 15 software prices of £249 and £149 a screen, assuming one screen per 8,000 patients and excluding hardware, so a practice with kiosks from Jayex or similar will usually pay more. Patient registration add-ons such as Healthtech-1 price by population band on request, so they start at zero. The panic button, which Medicus includes as standard, is estimated from Little Green Button's G-Cloud 15 price per device per month (£4.16 for up to 10 devices, £3.30 up to 25, £2.50 up to 50, £2.00 up to 200, billed annually), assuming one device per 500 patients; EDGEALERT lists £195 a practice site a year for up to 100 devices on G-Cloud 15, so a practice on that product should enter £195. Document scanning, referral letters and ICE ordering have no public price and are usually funded by the ICB, the sending trust or the pathology provider, so they start at zero. Clinical templates and searches are not listed: Medicus integrates with Ardens rather than replacing it.

  • PCN hub

    For a PCN or federation, Medicus includes a shared hub organisation at no extra charge. The equivalent on EMIS is typically charged at £15,000 to £30,000 a year, a figure supplied by Medicus's commercial team rather than a published list price, so the lower end is used by default and should be replaced with the network's actual figure where known. It is counted once for the whole network, not per practice, and is shown as a saving to the PCN or federation unless "Paid by" is changed.

  • Who pays

    Each add-on carries a "Paid by" setting: the practice, the PCN or federation itself (for a network estimate), or an ICB, trust or other commissioner. The defaults reflect common arrangements (messaging, online consultation, SMS, document management, ICE and referral tools are usually commissioned centrally; kiosks, calling screens, panic buttons and registration tools are usually bought by the practice), and should be corrected for the practice's own contracts. The results list a direct saving for each organisation separately, because a saving on an add-on belongs to whoever pays for it and should be presented to them.

Important information

  1. What this is. An illustrative estimate of the staff time a GP practice, or a group of practices, might release if it moved to Medicus, and of the add-on costs it might stop paying. It is a planning aid, not a quotation, forecast, offer or guarantee, and it does not form part of any contract.
  2. Where the figures come from. The time saved per login, per clinical session and in administrative work is the reported experience of one NHS practice of about 12,000 patients that uses Medicus. It has not been independently audited. Results for other practices, and for networks and regions, are scaled from it in proportion to the figures entered and will vary with list size, staffing, hardware, network and ways of working. Figures for a PCN, federation or ICB are aggregates across its practices, not a prediction for any one of them. Every figure is presented as "up to": the top of the range the inputs imply, not a central forecast.
  3. Time, not money. Released time is shown as hours and is not converted into a cash value. How it is used is for the practice to decide, and the model makes no assumption about it; it becomes a cash saving only if hours, overtime or locum spend actually fall.
  4. Third-party costs. Add-on prices marked "est." are publicly listed prices (Crown Commercial Service G-Cloud and supplier price lists, dated in Basis and limits) scaled to list size. Actual costs depend on the practice's own contracts and may be lower or higher. Who pays for each add-on is recorded as entered, and each saving is shown against the organisation that pays, because it belongs to them. The PCN hub figure is the typical annual charge for an equivalent hub on another system, as advised by Medicus, not a published price. Figures typed in are used as entered and are not verified.
  5. Third-party names. AccuRx, Docman, Patchs, eConsult, EMIS, Healthtech-1, Little Green Button, EDGEALERT, Engage and ICE are trademarks of their respective owners, which are not connected with Medicus. They are named only to identify systems a practice may already use. Whether Medicus replaces a given system for a given practice depends on that practice's requirements and should be confirmed before any decision.
  6. Your inputs. Results depend on the figures entered and any assumptions changed, all of which are recorded in the PDF report. Check them before relying on the output.
  7. Not advice. Nothing here is financial, procurement, clinical or legal advice. Any business case should be built on the practice's own measured data and follow the practice's and its ICB's governance.
  8. Accuracy and liability. Reasonable care has been taken in building the model, but Medicus and the authors of this calculator accept no liability for decisions made in reliance on it. Figures are correct to the dates stated in Basis and limits and may change. Model version 1.3, prices and assumptions reviewed November 2026.
